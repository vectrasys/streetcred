Port Calloway

A small harbor city on the Central Coast, built by whalers, sardine canners and lumbermen, with a pine forest on the hill and fog most summer mornings. About 24,800 people live here across seven neighborhoods, each built in a different era.

Port Calloway is a fictional city created for a hackathon demo. Its street layout comes from real OpenStreetMap geometry; every name, date and person on this site is invented.

A short history

Before 1849
The shore of the bay was home to Ohlone (Rumsen) communities for thousands of years. Shell mounds were found under Front Street when the wharf was rebuilt in 1911.
1849
Captain Elias Calloway builds a supply wharf for ships heading to the gold fields. Stores, a customs shed and a boarding house grow around it: today’s Old Wharf.
1868
Port Calloway is incorporated as a town. Population: 412.
1875
A Methodist summer camp meeting rents tent lots in the pine grove. Families replace the tents with tiny board-and-batten cottages: the start of Chautauqua Grove.
1882
The Calloway & Sons lumber mill opens on the creek flats. The mill closes in 1958, but the area keeps its name, Millworks.
1908–1946
The sardine boom. Twelve canneries and the barrel makers who supply them fill the waterfront with brick warehouses: The Cooperage.
1912
A streetcar line climbs the hill, and Craftsman bungalows follow it into the pines of Pine Heights.
1919–1938
After the Front Street fire, the city rebuilds its civic center in Spanish Colonial Revival style. City Hall opens on Mission Plaza in 1927.
1947–1952
The sardines disappear. The canneries close one by one.
1952–1968
Post-war families buy ranch houses on the former dairy land of Seaview Terrace.
1979
The Cooperage becomes a historic district; canneries turn into lofts, studios and restaurants.
2019–2024
Public Works installs street cameras for traffic and storm-drain monitoring, now a network of more than 600 cameras.
August 2026
StreetCred launches: residents fix small problems in their neighborhood, the cameras confirm the result, and the city pays them through PayPal.
