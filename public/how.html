The program

How it works

Small problems — a dumped mattress, graffiti, a blocked storm drain — get fixed faster when neighbors can do it. The city pays for the result, and its street cameras confirm that the work was done.

The four steps

1

Pick a task

Open the map and claim a task near you. The spot is reserved for you for 48 hours. You can hold up to 3 tasks.
2

Show your code

When you arrive, hold the one-time QR code from My account up to the camera for a few seconds.
3

Do the work

Bag it, paint it, sweep it. Gloves and bags are free at the Public Works yard, 1400 Mill Road.
4

Get paid

Tap I’m done. Once the result is confirmed, the reward goes to your PayPal account in the next daily batch.

See a problem that is not on the map? Click the nearest camera and choose Report a problem here. Public Works reviews reports within one business day and sets the reward.

Technical details for judges
  • Money in: PayPal Checkout with the official JS SDK buttons; Orders API v2, created and captured on the server. The amount comes from the server, a capture is counted once.
  • Money out: PayPal Payouts, one batch per business day at 5 pm PT; sender_batch_id is the idempotency key. Venmo items use recipient_wallet: "Venmo" with a US mobile number.
  • Books: double-entry ledger — sponsor funds → program budget → payable → in transit at PayPal → paid out or returned.
  • Statuses: taken from the Payouts API; webhooks are logged and never trusted without signature verification.
  • Treasury Copilot: PayPal Agent Toolkit in the sandbox; refunds and invoices are proposals that a human approves.
  • Verification: the camera (edge) reads the QR code and compares frames; the server checks the one-time code and thresholds and asks a vision model about the cropped work zone.

How cameras check the work

The camera compares the task area before and after. It does not look at people.

  1. Your code. The camera reads your one-time QR code. It proves that the person who claimed the task was there. Each code works once.
  2. Same view. The frames before and after must come from the same camera angle, so an old or edited photo cannot be used.
  3. Real change in the task area. The area of the task must change clearly, while the rest of the frame stays the same. Changes in lighting are corrected first, so evening light cannot “erase” the trash.
  4. An AI check of the area. A vision model looks only at the cropped task area and answers one question: is it clean? It must see a problem before and a clean area after.

If every check passes, the deed is approved automatically. If anything is uncertain, a Public Works reviewer looks at the footage, usually within one business day. A clear mismatch is rejected; you can ask for a human review within 14 days.

Payments

When you get paid

Approved rewards are sent once every business day. The cut-off is 5:00 pm Pacific: deeds approved before then are paid the same evening, later ones the next business day. Payments go out as one PayPal Payouts batch; PayPal usually delivers within minutes, and you get an email from PayPal for each payment.

Where the money goes

To the wallet you choose in My account → Payout settings: your PayPal account (by email) or your Venmo account (by US mobile number). You receive the full reward: the city pays the payout fee. Rewards are paid in US dollars. If you have no account yet, PayPal or Venmo sends you a link to claim the money.

How the city keeps the books

Every reward moves through a double-entry ledger: approved → sent to PayPal → paid (or returned). A batch is sent with a unique ID, so a retry can never pay anyone twice. Payment statuses are taken from PayPal itself, never from unverified messages.

Taxes

StreetCred rewards are taxable income. The city asks for a Form W-9 once your rewards reach $600 in a calendar year; rewards above that amount are held — not lost — until the form is on file. The city’s finance office issues any tax forms the law requires. We cannot give tax advice.

Camera privacy

  • No facial recognition. The program never identifies people. Your QR code, not your face, links you to a task.
  • Task area only. The AI sees only a crop of the task area, not the whole street.
  • Short retention. Frames used to check a task are kept for 30 days for disputes, then deleted. Approved deeds keep only the result and a change map, not the images.
  • No selling or sharing. Frames and personal data are not sold and are shared only with PayPal to make a payment.

FAQ

Do I need a PayPal account?

You need an email address. If it has no PayPal account, PayPal emails you a link to create one and claim the money within 30 days.

Can I do a task without claiming it?

No. Claiming gives you the one-time code that proves the work was yours, and it stops two people from doing the same job.

What if the camera is offline?

Tasks are only offered at cameras that are online. If a camera goes offline after you claim, your claim is extended until it is back, or a reviewer checks the result on site.

Is anything too dangerous to do?

Yes: needles, chemicals, asbestos, anything on a roadway with traffic, and anything heavier than two people can lift safely. Report it and Public Works sends a crew.

Why was my deed rejected?

Usually because the area did not change enough, or the camera did not read the code. Open the task in My account to see which check failed, and ask for a human review if you think it is wrong.

Is there a limit?

Rewards are $5–$75 per task, and you can hold up to 3 tasks at once. The program has a monthly budget; when it is used up, new tasks open the next month.

Program terms

  1. Open to adults (18+) who live or work in Port Calloway and have a valid email address.
  2. Work only on public property: sidewalks, alleys, parks and public walls. Never enter private property.
  3. Rewards are set by Public Works before a task is published and are final.
  4. Fraud attempts — reusing a code, staging a mess, editing frames — lead to removal from the program and repayment of rewards.
  5. Participants are volunteers, not employees or contractors of the city.
  6. The city may change or end the program with 30 days’ notice; approved rewards are always paid.
