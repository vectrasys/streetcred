StreetCred · pilot template

Run a six-week pilot.

For a campus, an HOA or any organization with cameras already watching shared spaces. A pilot answers one question with your own numbers: does paying for verified small fixes beat waiting for a crew?

This is a proposed template. StreetCred has no pilot customers yet, and the figures below are an example budget for you to set — not results.

Scope

  • One site: a campus, an HOA or a business park.
  • Start with 2–3 cameras you already operate, pointed at shared outdoor areas; 10–20 cameras is a possible phase 2.
  • Small, safe tasks only: litter, bagged trash, boxes, flyers, weeds, graffiti on surfaces you own.
  • 5–10 participants to start: adult (18+) residents, students or employees who opt in.

Conditions

  • Work only on property you control; never on private lots or roads with traffic.
  • Hazards (needles, chemicals, heavy items) are reported, not touched.
  • Cameras check the work area, not people: no facial recognition. Proposed policy: frames for a task are kept 30 days for disputes, then deleted.
  • One-time QR check-in per claimed task; a claim reserves the spot for 48 hours.
  • Rewards over your tax threshold need a Form W-9 before more payouts (US); your finance team decides the threshold.

Timeline

  1. Week 0Pick cameras, outline work areas, set the reward pool and limits, brief your team.
  2. Week 1Soft launch on 2–3 cameras with 5–10 invited participants; publish the first tasks.
  3. Weeks 2–5Keep the same cameras and participants; payouts every business day; weekly review of rejected and disputed deeds.
  4. Week 6Stop new tasks, pay what is approved, compare the numbers below with your usual cleanup process and decide on phase 2 (10–20 cameras).

Review and disputes

  • Proposed review SLA: a reviewer decides deeds the camera and AI are unsure about within 2 business days.
  • Proposed appeal policy: a participant can ask for a human review within 14 days of a decision.
  • A reviewer sees the same before/after frames, the outlined area, every check and the AI answer.
  • Approved deeds join the next payout batch; delivery status comes from PayPal. A returned or unclaimed payout is re-issued only after a human reviews the item and the wallet.

What to measure

None of these are known yet; the pilot exists to measure them.

  • Tasks published, claimed, approved, rejected and sent to human review.
  • Time from a reported problem to a verified fix.
  • How often a reviewer overturns the camera and AI decision.
  • Cost per verified fix (rewards + fees + staff time) against your usual process.
  • Payouts that did not arrive and why (PayPal status).
